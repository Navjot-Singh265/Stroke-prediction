An end-to-end machine-learning project on 5,109 patients, plus a controlled experiment that measures how much common evaluation errors inflate results on this widely used Kaggle dataset. By Navjot Singh.

Approach

A leakage-free pipeline

Every step that learns from data lives inside the training fold, and the final test set keeps the natural 5% stroke rate.

      Interpretation

      What drives stroke risk

      Adjusted odds ratios from a multivariable logistic regression (log scale, 95% CI). Values right of the dashed line raise risk.

      🎯

      Four features are not enough

      A published claim that age, heart disease, glucose and hypertension suffice loses accuracy under leakage-free testing; BMI adds more than heart disease.

      👥

      Marital status is an age proxy

      It looks important alone, but is not significant once age is accounted for.

      ⚠️

      Missing BMI is an artefact

      Patients with no recorded BMI have about four times the stroke rate, likely a recording artefact, not a risk factor.

      Recommendations

      A reporting checklist

      For anyone publishing results on this dataset.

      • Split first; resample, impute, scale and select features only inside the training data.
      • Test on the natural stroke rate and report ROC-AUC and PR-AUC with intervals.
      • Always give the “never predict stroke” baseline (95.1% accuracy) and an age-only model.
      • Pick thresholds and settings from training data only, and publish the exact pipeline order.
